You can use it right now for: real rides between people you know, on your own phones, with cash payment. Everything works: booking, route choice, matching, tracking, chat, calls, PIN check, SOS, receipts, ratings, driver earnings, admin panel.

You cannot yet use it for: taking real card payments from strangers, or putting it on the Play Store. Both need accounts in your name and a bit of paperwork. Nothing more needs to be coded for the payment part; the code is written and waiting for your keys.

4. Free vs paid, in plain words

Free forever, no account, no card

Everything below costs nothing and will keep costing nothing, no matter how many people use it.

  • The app itself. All the code is yours. There is no licence fee and nothing to renew.
  • The database. SQLite is a free file on your server.
  • Maps. OpenStreetMap tiles are free and need no key.
  • Push notifications. I generated the keys on your machine. No Firebase account, no bill, ever.
  • The Android app file. Android Studio is free and so is building an APK. You can send that file to as many people as you like.
  • Running it on your own PC. Free. Only your electricity.
  • The website version. Free to serve from your own machine or a server you already have.
  • The test payment gateway. Free. You can practise the entire wallet flow without any payment account.

Free to start, costs money only when you grow

  • Route and address servers. The public ones are free but politely ask you not to use them heavily. Roughly speaking, a few hundred rides a day is fine. Beyond that you rent a small server (about ₹800 a month) and run your own copy. The software is free; you pay only for the machine.
  • Hosting. Your PC is free but must stay on. A small always-on server costs around ₹400 to ₹800 a month.
  • A public web address. A free tunnel gives you a temporary address that changes each time. A proper domain like swiftride.in costs about ₹900 a year.

Costs money from day one, if you want that feature

  • Taking card or UPI payments. Razorpay is free to open and has no monthly fee. They keep about 2% of each payment. On a ₹300 ride that is ₹6. You do need business documents (PAN, bank account, and usually GST) before they let you take live payments.
  • SMS one-time codes. Roughly 15 to 25 paise per SMS in India. A thousand sign-ups is around ₹200.
  • Google Play Store. A one-time $25, about ₹2,100, forever. Not a subscription.
  • Apple App Store. $99 a year, about ₹8,300, and you need a Mac to build it.
  • Call relay server. Only needed if you want in-app calls to work between two different mobile networks. Free software on a rented server (₹400 a month), or a pay-per-use service.

The cheapest honest path: keep cash payments, run it on your own PC with a free tunnel, hand out the APK file directly. That is ₹0 a month and you can carry real passengers tomorrow. Add the paid parts one at a time, only when you actually need them.

6. What I need from you

Nothing here is urgent. The app works without all of it. This is the list for when you want to go further.

Decisions only you can make

  1. The name. "SwiftRide" is my placeholder. Changing it is one line in one file, so tell me the real name whenever you pick it. Some options I suggested: Waypoint, Apna Rasta, Marg, Meterly, TrueFare, Pathly.
  2. The real fares. Tell me your city and what you think a ride should cost, or edit them yourself in the admin panel under Fares. Right now they are guesses.
  3. Your commission. Currently 15% of each fare. Say the word and I will change it.
  4. Cancellation rules. Currently: 2 minutes free, then ₹30 for a Go ride; drivers lose ₹20 for cancelling. Change any of those numbers if you disagree.
  5. Do drivers need approval? Right now new drivers can start immediately. One setting makes them wait for you to approve their documents. Which do you want?

Accounts to open, when you are ready

  1. Razorpay — only if you want card and UPI payments. Sign up at razorpay.com, complete the business KYC, then send me the two keys (Key ID and Key Secret). I paste them into one file and live payments are on. Do not put those keys in a chat message that others can read.
  2. An SMS provider (MSG91 or Twilio) — only if you want phone verification.
  3. A Google Play developer account — $25 once, only if you want Play Store distribution. You will also need a privacy policy page and some screenshots, which I can prepare.
  4. A hosting account — only when you want it online without your PC. Tell me your budget and I will pick one and set it up.

Things to test and tell me about

  1. Install the new APK on two phones and run a real ride end to end. Tell me anything that feels wrong.
  2. Try the SOS button and the share-trip link. Confirm you are happy with what a stranger holding the link can see.
  3. Try an in-app call between two phones on mobile data, not Wi-Fi. Tell me if the audio connects. That answers whether you need the relay server.
  4. Open the admin panel and change a fare. Confirm the new price shows up in the app.

8. How to run and test it

On your PC

cd C:\Projects\Ideas\Uber
npm run install:all     # first time only
npm run seed            # creates the demo accounts
npm run serve           # website + app + API on http://localhost:4000

On your phones

Install dist-apk/SwiftRide-release.apk. Make sure the phone is on the same Wi-Fi as the PC. The login screen already has your PC's address filled in. For testing away from home, run npm run tunnel on the PC and paste the printed address into the app.

The one thing that trips people up

A ride needs a driver who is online, of the same vehicle class, and within 3 km of the pickup. If nothing happens after you book, open the driver app, tap "Go online", and switch on "Simulate GPS" so the car sits at a sensible place. The seeded drivers are all in central Delhi.
